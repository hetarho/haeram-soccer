- limits: needs update-ssot. The conflicts, each with options for the planner:
  - 1950s matchday 60%+ against the 2000s top-flight mix (ECON-26). Distributions and ticket presets share one index, so a club's mix differs between eras only by its gate and sponsor slots. The reference club reaches the second tier (FRA, GER; ESP's lowest professional division) and, in ESP and some FRA/GER careers, the top flight within the 1950s, with crowds of 1.8–2.7k. The top-flight distribution the 2000s mix needs (60–85k units a season against 44–64k units of matchday income) leaves those seasons at 3–6% matchday in the top flight and 10–28% in the second tier, and weighted by turnover one top-flight season counts as much as 25–60 amateur ones: 21–35% (r1–r5). With the integrated distribution it is 52%, and the 2000s top flight is then 66% matchday. Options: distributions on a broadcasting index that outgrows ticket prices from the 1960s, or the 1950s mix judged below the top two divisions or on an established club.
  - ECON-9 second- and third-tier attendance on the reference club. Its gate cannot exceed its local supporters, which follow reputation (→ECON-23). At the close of its third-tier seasons it has 4.0–4.6k (r0, r1, r3–r5) against gates of 2.2–2.6k; with price ignored, average form brings 87% of them, 3.5–4.0k at best against the 4k floor. Second tier: 8.0–13.4k supporters, gates 4.1–6.9k (51–57% turnout at the 0.05 preset); 8k needs 60–100% at every home game. Options: measure the divisional targets on established clubs, or let a promotion move supporters toward the division's catchment.
  - wage bill, net result and the ladder against solvency (ECON-30, CLUB-26, ECON-12). The reference club outgrows its division: XI +10 to +14 above its league from the 1980s with level-20 facilities, and player sales are 33–91% of top-flight turnover.

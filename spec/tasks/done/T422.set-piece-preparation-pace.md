## goal
Draw the dead interval before a corner, free kick or goal kick — the ball being placed and the players walking to their places — in about 1.5 real seconds at every speed, with routes scaled by one factor and nothing else on the timeline changed.

## acceptance
- [v] `presentationClock.ts` derives the preparation intervals from frame data both models already carry: for each restart event (`kind: 'restart'`, `restart` corner, freeKick or goalKick) the running-time span from the ball going dead to that restart's kick. A test on one fixture of each model asserts where a corner's interval begins and ends.
- [v] The presentation clock's state is running match seconds. Running play advances `speed × 60 / WATCH_MINUTE_MS` seconds per real ms; inside a preparation of D seconds it advances at the larger of that rate and `D / SET_PIECE_PREPARATION_MS` (new export, 1500). Seeking (WEB-23), speed changes and the goal hold (WEB-94) keep their behaviour in match time.
- [v] `presentationClock.test.ts` cases: "a 32 s corner preparation takes 1.5 real s at 1x and 2x"; "at 16x a preparation shorter at running speed keeps running speed"; "throw-ins, kick-offs and penalties keep running speed"; "a speed change inside a preparation keeps the match second"; "seeking into a preparation lands on that match second"; "frames, samples and ball times are read, never changed".
- [v] Goal feedback and the possession cue read the running-seconds clock; their existing cases pass with the new clock.
- [v] `set-piece-defence.spec.ts` and `stable-controls-and-pacing.spec.ts` assert the compressed preparation (players still travel, no position jump) for the batch run.
- [v] Task checks (ARCH-30): typecheck, lint, format:check, `vitest run --changed`, `pnpm run build`.

## impl notes
- Presentation only (MATCH-7, MATCH-12): no engine, record, frame or seed change, and the presented clock simply runs through the preparation faster; the added time and settled facts stay the engine's.
- The ball going dead is the end of the last event before the restart (where `holders` turns '' or the samples' phase turns 'restart'); the kick is the restart event's own time. On the pre-feature path one trace step holds the whole preparation (for example a corner's 32 s dead and 3.2 s kick), on the candidate the participants arrive and wait; both compress the same way.
- Replace `presentationLength` and `clockAt`/`runningSeconds` by running-second functions where callers need them; the progress bar and seek map running seconds directly.
- Keep `SET_PIECE_PREPARATION_MS` the only number for the preparation; the owner reviews it in play before any further dead-ball compression (ideation/match-viewing-pace-and-freedom).

## result
- outcome: The presentation clock runs on running match seconds. `runningLength`/`runningRate` replace `presentationLength`/`clockAt`/`runningSeconds`. `setPiecePreparations(frames)` finds the corner, free kick and goal kick dead intervals. `presentationAdvance(seconds, ms, speed, length, preparations)` runs at max(running rate, D / `SET_PIECE_PREPARATION_MS` = 1500) inside a preparation, and `presentationMs` is its inverse. Goal feedback (`seconds` state and goal instants, `length`/`preparations` input) and `Pitch.tsx` (seek, progress, possession cue) read that clock; `possessionCue.ts` already took seconds into the minute and is unchanged. The pitch canvas publishes the presented second as `data-seconds` for browser journeys. No engine, record, frame or seed change. The ball goes dead at the end (`t`) of the last event before the restart, in the same period. On both models a restart event's `t` is where its delivery lands, not the kick (pre-feature 51′ corner: kick 43 s, landing 45 s; candidate scene corner: kick sample 17.43 s, landing at the receiver 19.7 s). So the kick is read from what comes before the landing: the taker (restart actor) taking the ball in `holders` (pre-feature), else the last `phase: 'restart'` sample with the ball still on the restart's spot (`from`) — the candidate's kick snapshot, where T359 starts the set-piece jobs. The spot check is a review fix: the delivery flies in the same phase and a minute's end samples it in the air, which had made that sample the "kick" and drawn part of a delivery at the preparation rate (1 of 80 restarts in the candidate set-piece-defence match; 0 after the fix). Either must fall more than 0.1 s before the landing, so a taker's self-pass is not read as the kick, and a restart without either keeps running speed. Every entry carrying `restart` corner, freeKick or goalKick counts, also the free kicks and goal kicks the engine records as the `foul` or `offside` their delivery ended in (its actor is then the fouler or receiver, so only the spot snapshot applies; 1–2 per candidate match, none pre-feature). This goes past the acceptance's literal `kind: 'restart'` because WEB-32 covers every free kick and goal kick. Reading the impl note's "kick = the restart event's own time" literally would also compress the delivery, which WEB-32 and MATCH-12 keep at the selected speed; on the pre-feature this reading gives the note's own "32 s dead, 3.2 s kick". Measured on four seeded first matches per model (probe-pace, set-piece-defence, set-piece-defence-2, stable-pace), after the review fixes: every prepared entry got a preparation (pre-feature 47/58/56/53, candidate 70/80/61/69), no kick sample lies off its spot, and kick→landing stays at running speed (1.0–3.6 s pre-feature, 0.15–5.6 s candidate). Dead→kick median was 27–28 s (pre-feature) and 22–24.5 s (candidate), range 16.9–55.8 s. A whole match at 1x/2x takes about 14.2–15.9 / 8.0–8.6 min, against 18.8–19.4 / 9.4–9.7 uniform (4x about 4.6–4.8, 16x 1.2, almost unchanged). Two autopilot decisions are handed to the owner's in-play review of `SET_PIECE_PREPARATION_MS` (ideation/match-viewing-pace-and-freedom), with the number left unchanged here: (a) the kick is read from the taker's take or the on-spot restart sample, not the restart event's own time; (b) the measured match lengths are slightly over WEB-32's "about 13–14 / 7 minutes".

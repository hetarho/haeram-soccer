## goal
One shared painter draws a posed top-down human figure wearing a `WornKit` or `WornKeeper`, with size-dependent detail and readable number tags, for match observation and kit previews alike.

## acceptance
- [v] `createFigurePainter()` returns `{ draw(ctx, pose, kit, options) }`. It draws, bottom to top: shadow, legs with socks and boots, shorts, arms with sleeves in shirt colours and skin or gloves, torso, and the hair-covered head with a small facing cue (MATCH-36).
- [v] The torso shows the shirt's pattern and trim through `figureShirtColourAt` and `FIGURE_TORSO` from `kit-patterns.ts`, so painted colours agree with what selection samples (KIT-7). All eight patterns paint distinctly at the large sample size.
- [v] Detail reduces in steps by torso width in device pixels (`FIGURE_LOD`). The smallest step keeps team colours, facing and limb silhouettes (MATCH-36 scale rule).
- [v] `playerLook(id)` returns a stable skin tone and hair colour and style from a palette by ID hash. It carries no gameplay meaning (MATCH-36).
- [v] `numberTags.ts`: `placeNumberTags(figures, bounds)` deterministically picks, for each figure, one of eight non-rotating offsets with the least overlap with other tags, figures, the ball and the carrier cue; `drawNumberTag(ctx, tag, options)` draws it readably on every theme.
- [v] Painting draws only. It never changes a pose, position or kit. Static parts may be cached up to `FIGURE_SPRITE_LIMIT` entries keyed by kit, detail step and DPR.
- [v] Unit tests in `figurePainting.test.ts` (recording 2D context in the style of `kitPainting.test.ts`): "draws layers in order", "torso sample points use the shirt geometry colours", "keeper draws gloves", "detail steps by torso width", "same look for the same id", "cache is bounded".
- [v] Unit tests in `numberTags.test.ts`: "tags never rotate", "crowded figures get distinct offsets", "placement is deterministic".
- [v] ARCH-30 task tier passes (typecheck, lint, format:check, changed unit tests) and `pnpm run build`.

  - `{ x, y }`: the centre in CSS px.
  - `radius`: half the torso width in CSS px. Callers pass today's `markerRadius(width)` so the torso keeps the current marker scale (MATCH-36).
  - `dpr`, `look: PlayerLook`.
  - `outline`, `outlineWidth`: theme skin tokens (WEB-91).
  - `ball?`: its socket drawn by the caller.
  - A `WornKeeper` paints gloves on the hands.
- `FIGURE_LOD`: steps by torso width in device px, numbers defined here. ≥ 40 px is full (hair strands, sleeve cuffs, boot detail, shading); 18–40 px drops the strands and cuffs; below 18 px leaves flat colour areas, the pattern's largest regions, limb outlines and the facing cue.

- `PlayerLook { skin; hair; style }`. The palettes are defined here; the skin palette covers a broad, natural range. The hash is a local FNV-1a.
- `numberTags.ts`:

  - Text and backplate come from the pitch skin tokens the caller passes, so every theme stays readable.

## result
- outcome: `figurePainting.ts`: `createFigurePainter(factory?)` returns `{ draw(ctx, pose, kit, options), size }`. Draw order: screen-space shadow; the silhouette (every part grown by `outlineWidth` in `outline`); legs (skin, socks, boots); shorts; arms (skin, sleeves, hands or gloves); the torso sprite; the head (skin, ears, nose as the facing cue, hair). With `pose.overhead` the arms paint after the head. The body frame is `translate(x, y)`, `rotate(facing + π/2)`, `scale(2·radius·(1 + 0.5·jump))`, with forward up and the player's right +X, so `FIGURE_TORSO`'s unit square maps straight onto it. The sprite paints the field, the `shirtPatternRegions` polygons, the sleeve bands (`sleeveTrim`) and the collar ring (`collar` ellipse minus the head circle, even-odd), then translucent shading above `flat`. Sleeves take the pattern colour covering most of the torso column at u 0.1 / 0.9 (halves: right sleeve in the second colour); cuffs in trim at full. `options.ball(ctx)` is called in the caller's space at the socket's layer: over the legs and shorts for a ball at the feet, over the head for one held high, last when the pose has no socket. `figurePoint(pose, point, { x, y, radius })` gives a socket's CSS px in that same frame. `lying` deepens the torso by up to 0.5. `FIGURE_LOD { full: 40, medium: 18 }` and `figureDetail(radius, dpr)`: full adds hair strands, cuffs, limb edges and glove seams; medium keeps the shading, ears and boot laces; flat keeps flat areas, the same pattern/trim geometry, the silhouette and a larger nose. `FIGURE_SPRITE_LIMIT` 128, FIFO, keyed by shirt fields, detail, sprite pixel width and DPR; sprites are painted at 2× device px so turning keeps their edges. `playerLook(id)`: FNV-1a with an avalanche finaliser over `SKIN_TONES` (10, light to dark), `HAIR_COLOURS` (darker ones repeated; the six darkest skins draw from the seven darkest hair entries) and `HAIR_STYLES` (crop, short, curly, swept, buzz). `numberTags.ts`: `placeNumberTags(inputs, { width, height, avoid })` takes figures in ID order. Each gets the `TAG_OFFSETS` entry (8 screen directions, nearest point `1.1·radius + 2` px from its centre) whose box costs least by `TAG_CONFIG.weights` (other tags 8, avoid circles 6, other figures' footprint 3, off the area 10, +0.5 per preference step). Results come back in input order on whole px, clamped inside the area. Also `numberTagSize`, and `numberTagStyle(skin, family)` (plate = `markerOutline`, digits and edge = `ground`). `drawNumberTag(ctx, tag, style)` draws a rounded plate and the number upright, with no transform of its own.

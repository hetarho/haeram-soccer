## goal
At a set-piece kick, the candidate's urgent defensive pairing no longer takes a man-marker off the runner his place names; an unmarked runner gets the nearest defender who is not already marking someone.

## impl notes
- From T366's limit: at a kick, `defendSetPiece` gives each scene marker a committed mark; the same instant's `updateIntentions` meets an urgent danger (`inDanger`, as after a short corner) and pairs each unmarked runner with his nearest free man, where a committed mark counts as free, so a marker can leave his man for another runner who then waits for a later update. Seen three times in `set-piece-1` under T365's setting and twice under T345's. Read T366's and T348's records.
- This changes candidate football after T323's reference; T286's final registry re-measures the combined source, so record before/after here and let T286 judge the release gate.

- limits:
  - (1) The urgent press at the kick, unchanged by this task's acceptance, still takes markers off their runners. `inDanger` reads the man the restart leaves on the ball, and a delivery that gives nobody else the ball (a header, a ball out) leaves the taker there. The kick's own update therefore sends the nearest defender, with no reach limit, to press the taker at the corner flag or the free kick's spot while the ball is in the air. The press also counts a committed mark as free.
    - In the census, 311 of 445 urgent presses at a kick went to the taker (209 after a header or shot, 92 after a ball out, 10 at short corners).
    - 219 of those 311 came from a man-marker whose runner still held his box spot, about one delivery in seven.
    - The header is resolved at the kick, so this moves second-ball positions and playback, not the delivery's outcome.
    - Recorded in §4.7 for a candidate football task (see the final report).
  - (2) A runner with no unmarked defender within 20 m stays unmarked until a later update rather than taking a marker.
  - (3) The census and the playback case find man-markers by their kick places (`setPiece.mark` goal-side of a runner's spot), which is what `setPieceTargets` writes, not by reading the scene itself.

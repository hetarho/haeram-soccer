## goal
Remove automatic situation pacing and draw the engine's timed ball/player paths continuously.

## acceptance
- [v] `presentationClock` uses WEB-32's uniform mapping through build-up, dead balls, chances, added time and extra time; candidate `pace` is neutral and old frame weights cannot introduce situation slowdown.
- [v] Playback emits T273's explicit semantic knot times. Pitch and replay use elapsed match time to locate irregular ball/player samples, never shorten a flight, and never interpolate across a declared period or seek transition.
- [v] Changing speed preserves the current match-time position. Detailed seek pauses as before, speed selection resumes live observation, hidden-tab behavior and archived controls remain correct, and result/calendar facts do not change.
- [v] Clock/playback tests cover `spends equal time on equal match intervals`, `changes speed without a time jump`, `keeps dead-ball and open-play multipliers equal`, `renders arrival before the next kick`, `preserves minute-boundary continuity`, and `reads legacy frames without inventing missing detail`.
- [v] Preserve actual kit patterns, crests and goalkeeper appearance from T253. Update the existing pacing/core Chromium journeys for restarts and speed changes; implement the specs and list them for the batch run, with no browser run in this task.
- [v] Task checks and production build follow ARCH-30; note the affected browser specs in result limits.

## result
- outcome: Done. `presentationClock.ts` is now uniform (→WEB-32): every frame is one running minute of 4000 ms at 1x (`presentationLength`, `presentationAdvance`, `runningSeconds`/`clockAt`), frame `pace` is never read, and the speed multiplies only the clock's advance, so a speed change keeps the match-time position. `runningTime` places a sample at its recorded elapsed seconds on timed frames (`startSeconds`) and at equal shares of the minute on legacy frames; `presentationSamples` builds the player samples and the ball track with their running times; `locate` finds an irregular sample by time and holds instead of interpolating across a period change. `Pitch.tsx` draws players and the ball from these (the ball from the track at its own times, so flights keep their recorded length); seek, detailed-seek pause, speed-resumes-live, hidden-tab, archived replay controls, kits, crests and keepers are unchanged. In `grid/playback.ts`, candidate traces (`setup.model`) build the ball from T274's knots at their recorded times (retrieval flight and set-down, rest on the spot, flights from kick to touch/receive/arrive, carries and tackles), with no flight scaling and no fixed taker walk: the taker walks to the spot at `PLAYBACK_CONFIG.takerSpeed` (6 m/s), no earlier than the ball is placed and no later than his kick, and the open-play actor holds until his kick through timed waypoints (`Path.route`). Candidate frames carry `startSeconds` (each minute 60 s after the last in its period), `ballTimes` (80 regular points plus every knot), player samples at snapshots and waypoints at least `PLAYBACK_CONFIG.sampleSpacing` (2 s) apart, a drawn sample at each period's whistle, event `t` in real seconds and `pace = NEUTRAL_FRAME_PACE`; `frameTimeIssues` reports none. Pre-feature frames are unchanged (8 samples, 80 points, their pace weights kept but unread). `replay.ts` archive frames use `NEUTRAL_FRAME_PACE`. The e2e journeys `stable-controls-and-pacing.spec.ts` and `match-core.spec.ts` now check exactly 4 s per minute at 1x, the presented minute kept across each speed change, and a steady 250 ms per minute at 16x through restarts.

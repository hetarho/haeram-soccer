# MATCH Observable match simulation
> r67 | Define one complete, calibrated probabilistic result for every match and, for the own club's matches, a script and a staged picture drawn from that result.

## decisions
- MATCH-1 [o] Every match is settled once, as one joint probabilistic draw of its whole record (→!MATCH-22/26/27): two halves of 45 minutes plus added time, and extra time and a shoot-out where →MATCH-6 calls for them. The record is the only source of match facts; an own match's script and picture (→!MATCH-38/39) are made from it and never change it. ← one way of settling a match for every club, and a picture that cannot disagree with the record
  - added time is the time each half lost to goals, substitutions, injuries, cards and set-piece delays, announced as the half ends; the modern era plays about 6–12 minutes a match in all (EPL 2023-24 11:39, the five seasons before about 7:20).
  - at half-time the sides change ends and the manager may substitute (→MATCH-20).
- MATCH-2 [o] Outcome factors include the players' attributes at their slots, club/manager quality, fatigue, home advantage, and the actually applied tactical preset; the one exception to equal terms is the owner's 승리의 부적 (→OWNER-6). ← the own club must play on the same terms as the clubs it meets
  - every club's strength is its actual starting XI in its formation (→STAFF-22): the average 실효 OVR at the slots (→PLAYER-6) minus (fatigue − 20)/6 for fatigue above 20; an own player's 실효 OVR carries his adaptation multiplier (→PLAYER-10), a player of a club nobody owns his club's 조직력 multiplier (→LEAGUE-12).
  - every club adds (manager ability − 50)/4 for its manager — the own club's hired one, a club nobody owns its named manager (→STAFF-17) — and (morale − 60)/10, a club nobody owns at the neutral 60 since it tracks no morale; the own club also adds its manager style's strength (→STAFF-14) and at home its build's home strength (→ECON-22). ← a manager counts the same for every club, so the own club's edge is only what its manager, morale and build earn
  - fatigue up to 20 is ordinary match fitness and costs nothing in ratings, duels or tactic fit; only the excess weakens a player.
  - clubs nobody owns do not track fatigue match by match, so a pressing club's squad carries fatigue 32.
  - a strength gap counts with diminishing returns (gap / ⁴√(1 + (gap/24)⁴): 20→18.1, 30→22.0, 40→23.3); each side then adds its match-day form, a seeded fat-tailed draw (sd 7, within ±21) from a stream of its own, so watched and unwatched runs agree. ← even a far stronger side has bad days; a +20 favourite wins about 79%, draws 14% and loses 7%, as in real league data
  - home, manager and morale points and the form move every player's skills too (half the difference between the sides to each), so a strength point counts the same wherever it comes from.
  - calibration target: Elo with 16 Elo per rating point; the result path (→MATCH-22) holds it across gaps −10 to +40, the own club's fixtures included at home and away, so equal prepared strengths give the own club the same curve as every other club; each calibration records its measured error there. The executable target and comparison registry is `scripts/lib/match-parity.ts`; calibration measurements are historical evidence, not an additional rule source.
- MATCH-3 [o] Provide possession, goals, assists, shots, shots on target, attempted/completed passes, tackles, interceptions, dribbles, saves, fouls, yellow and red cards, corners, offsides, minutes played, substitutions, injuries and player contributions.
  - possession is a side's share of both sides' attempted passes (the published convention).
- MATCH-4 [o] Display counts, denominators and defined ratios; no attempts means no defined percentage. Compare own match, season and career totals.
- MATCH-5 [o] An observed own match exposes its script's every touch at its place, the action chain, cumulative xG per minute, timeline and progressive statistics (→!MATCH-38). Own matches of the current season keep their post-match material (→MATCH-25) until the season close; then each own player keeps one season heat map and the matches keep outcomes, highlights, each side's match xG and counters, never frame traces or per-match maps. ← bounded retention protects long careers (measured 261017, 100-season checkpoint: 1,564,446 bytes, about 99.5% of the then 1.5 MiB limit; current budget →SAVE-4)
- MATCH-6 [o] A cup match that must produce a winner and is level after 90 minutes plays extra time, two halves of 15 minutes on the same engine, then a penalty shootout kick by kick: five each, then sudden death. All scores are settled once.
  - each kick is the taker's 슈팅 and 침착성 against the keeper's 선방 (→PLAYER-2); the manager orders the takers best first.
- MATCH-7 [o] Fast and observed processing use the same match seed and football result path; animation speed, selected view and browser clock cannot reroll results. An own match's script and post-match material are written from its record at settlement the same way whether it is watched or not (→!MATCH-38).

- MATCH-8 [o] The four tactics are the manager's call (→STAFF-22); the owner has no tactical control. Each sets real behaviour on the pitch, and their tradeoffs keep: possession uses the midfielders' 패스 and 시야 but fewer direct shots; counter uses the defenders' 위치선정 and the forwards' 속도 and gains against press but sacrifices possession; press uses 체력 and freshness to force chances at higher fatigue cost; balanced retains defensive stability. ← the owner shapes play only through the manager he hires
  | tactic | formation | out of possession | in possession |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | possession | 4-3-3 | high line, mid-to-high block, counter-press after a loss | short passing, patient recycling, width held by the wingers, the inverted full-back steps into midfield |
  | press | 4-2-3-1 | high line, presses the opponent's build-up, counter-press after a loss | quick vertical passing after a regain |
  | counter | 4-4-2 | deep line, low-to-mid block, engages around halfway | direct forward passes and runs in behind straight after a regain |
  | balanced | 3-5-2 | mid block, engages in the middle third | mixed passing, wing-backs give the width |
  - no detailed instructions (line height, width, tempo, pressing intensity) are offered.
  - shared engine profiles expose fit rather than guaranteed win probabilities.

- MATCH-9 [o] The midfielders' 패스 affects possession and pass completion; the defenders' 태클, 위치선정 and 공중볼 suppress opposing shot creation; 체력 and fatigue limit press effectiveness. Any tactic fit shown (→CLUB-14) reads the same attributes and tactic behaviour the engine plays, never a separate formula.

- MATCH-10 [o] Mobile observation keeps score, pitch, latest event, pause/speed, live charts and the primary action visible together, including empty, live, finished, replay and new-match states. Stats, timeline, player inspection and the full league table open explicitly, while the standing after the match shows once it ends (→=WEB-50); presentation choices never reroll settled facts.

- MATCH-11 [o] Named players decide the record: who acts, who wins the ball and who scores is drawn from the attributes below (→!MATCH-16). ← individual players matter on the ball and against it
  - every attribute the draw reads is the player's own (→PLAYER-2) × his level at the slot (→PLAYER-4) × his adaptation or his club's 조직력 multiplier, with in-match fatigue; in-match fatigue grows with minutes, faster for low 체력 and under press.
  | recorded action | credited by |
  |---|---|
  | short or long pass | 패스 |
  | through ball | 시야 |
  | cross, cut-back | 크로스, a wide slot weighing more |
  | carry, take-on | 드리블 |
  | shot, header | 슈팅, 공중볼 for a header |
  | caught offside | low 판단력 (→MATCH-18) |
  | tackle, interception | 태클, 위치선정 |
  | penalty, free kick, corner | the manager's named taker (→MATCH-18) |
  - how many actions a side attempts and completes, the chances it makes and how they end come from both sides' strength, tactics and profiles (→MATCH-2/8/9); a penalty is the taker's 슈팅 and 침착성 against the keeper's 선방.
  - shot quality is the chance's place and situation: its cell, a header or a foot, open play, a corner, a free kick or a penalty; a place's level is real football's goal probability there (the per-cell rates of the engine's cell baselines).
  - recorded xG is that chance's goal probability for the match level's average finisher and keeper, so goals minus xG measures finishing and xGA minus goals against measures keeping.
  - the last completed pass before the shot is the key pass and earns the assist when the shot scores; a chance its shooter made alone, a penalty and a direct free kick have none.
  - player contributions record the actual passer, receiver, winner of the ball, shooter, keeper, fouler, carded player and substitute.
- MATCH-12 [o] Observed playback draws the staging of the match's script (→!MATCH-38/39): the ball flies along the scripted actions and players stand where the staging puts them. ← a ball that stops in open space and then jumps to an opponent reads as a bug, not football
  - the selected speed applies uniformly to running play, through build-up, chances and every restart's kick and delivery; 1x maps every 60 match seconds to 60 real seconds, real time (→WEB-32). Added time and extra time use the same mapping. The dead-ball beat (→WEB-32), goal-feedback holds (→WEB-94), seeking and the interval between periods are explicit presentation transitions outside running play; none changes a settled fact.
  - the minute shown is the record's: a goal, a shot, a card, an injury or a substitution is seen in the minute the record gives it. Between such moments the picture's own seconds are fitted to the match clock at one rate held near 1, so no passage is drawn visibly faster or slower than its neighbours (→!MATCH-39).
  - during dead time each player is drawn moving at his staged, movement-bounded pace until he reaches his place and then standing there; nobody glides across the whole dead interval on one straight line. ← players who hurry to their places and wait read as football; a crawl reads as a bug
  - the dead-ball beat shortens only its presentation time: the ball and players travel their staged routes in order, the rush scales their travel by one factor, the standing that follows is what the still beat shortens, and the staged dead time, added time and settled facts are unchanged.
  - a player on the ball moves with it: from receiving it until he plays it he takes it in stride at a carrying pace his movement rules bound, and the ball is played and the action resolved from where he has taken it, with every other player moving on his own (→MATCH-17) meanwhile; under close pressure he shields it on the move, taking it away from the nearest opponent at the same bounded pace, and he stands on it only to hold it up or lay it off, as a keeper distributing it or as a restart's taker. ← a carrier who stops dead on every reception reads as a moving diagram, not football
    - a carrier commits to one forward escape direction or shields and holds; pressure never makes him circle around an opponent. Within 2.2 m of an outfield opponent he releases a scripted pass within 0.4 s when movement permits; a press-resistant player, whose (dribbling × 2 + composure + strength) / 4 is at least 78, may shield for up to 2.5 s. Action travel, recorded outcomes and movement bounds still hold.
    - only an outfield opponent makes him shield it: the keeper coming out to him is never a man he turns his back on and takes the ball away from; facing the keeper he keeps going at goal as the script has him: he shoots, takes him on or carries past him. ← a forward through on goal who backs away from the keeper reads as a bug
    - a shot is struck as soon as the script calls it: with nobody close to him he sets himself in a stride or two toward goal, never running the ball sideways or away from goal first. ← a forward alone in front of goal who dawdles until he is crowded reads as a bug
      - a first-time shot — a delivery's finish or a second ball's — is struck at once, never held back while whoever wins what it leaves loose runs there; that ball runs on from the man it meets. ← a finish that waits for the rebound's winner leaves the blocker drawn metres from the ball he blocked
  - action durations and restart preparation allow the ball and players to traverse their staged routes. Player speed, acceleration and turning are bounded by movement rules using pace and fatigue; kicks have bounded travel speed and a visible departure and arrival. No path is compressed to catch a later action or replaced by an instantaneous position assignment.
  - flight: a quadratic curve from launch to landing whose sideways bend grows with distance; passes of 18+ units rise with distance (up to 9, about 30% of long balls driven low), shots stay low; bend and height come from the motion stream only.
  - every flight ends where the script states it: at the receiver, the interceptor, the tackler, out of play or in the net. After the ball leaves play it returns to the real restart spot through a staged retrieval/placement interval; the taker and set-piece participants travel there before the kick. Substitutions and the change of ends have explicit entry/exit or period transitions.
  - ball and player paths preserve continuity at actions, restarts and minute boundaries. Sampling must resolve departures, arrivals and changes of intent; interpolation does not invent a football event the script does not state. The renderer draws the ball above its shadow, up to 35% larger when high.

- MATCH-15 [o] A recorded action carries what the result path drew for it — its cells, kind, set piece, outcome, whether it broke a line, who made it and who won the ball — and its script adds the phase of play it stands in: build-up, progression, final third, counter-attack or set piece. ← a tag nobody settled would be an invented fact

- MATCH-16 [o] Who performs each recorded action is drawn by probability from a weight table. ← realism is measurable against real data instead of feel; roles, traits and attributes make players differ
  - the base weight is his line's share of that action in that band of the pitch × his minutes on the pitch in the period; his role's habit (→PLAYER-5) and his play traits (→PLAYER-9) bend it, and the attribute the action reads (→!MATCH-11) raises it.
  - his place counts: the nearer his role stands (→MATCH-17's shape for a ball in that cell) to the cell where a pass, carry or take-on starts — for a tackle, interception, foul or offside the cell where it happens — the likelier the action is his, so a player's actions gather where he plays and a winger is not credited on the far flank. Each player's expected totals over the match stay what the weights without place give him. ← the script and the maps put him where the record says he acted (measured 261111 before the rule: his role's place a median 21 m from the cell, 54% beyond 20 m)
  - an action naming two men — the man who lost the ball and the one who won it (a tackle, an interception or a loose ball recovered), an offside's passer and the man caught, a foul's man fouled and its fouler — names two men on the pitch together in its period (→!MATCH-27): each side's men are drawn on their own weights, then matched across the action over the playing time they share, so a draw that can be matched keeps every man's count and draws nothing more; only a second man left without a partner is drawn again among the men on the pitch with the first, his weight scaled by the share of his spell the two have in common. ← the script plays every action while its men are on the pitch together (→~MATCH-38), and matching the drawn counts keeps each man's expected totals without refitting (measured 261111: 1 of 60 sampled matches credited a ball won across a substitution before the rule)
  - a shot's shooter and key passer are drawn among the players on the pitch in its minute by line, role, traits and attribute, without his place; the lines' shares of goals and the scorers' concentration hold →MATCH-23.
  - the manager's named takers take penalties, corners and the free kicks within shooting or crossing range (→MATCH-18).
  - a manager's school bends his side's profile (→STAFF-14), never one player's weight.
- MATCH-17 [o] In the picture every player moves as an individual within his side's tactic and phase (→!MATCH-39). ← coordinated movement makes roles and personalities readable in play
  - his place starts from his slot's shape for the phase — in possession, out of possession, transition — moved by his role's habit (→PLAYER-5, →STAFF-22), the block height and the ball-side shift; the side in possession keeps its rest defence.
  - he acts on his own moment: he reads a pass or a turnover after his own delay and follows his place at his own pace, so players neither start, stop nor turn as one. A settled turnover re-forms a side gradually; a counter-attack turns it at once.
  - teammates coordinate: a receiver shows while another opens a lane, one defender presses while another covers, each defender out of possession takes one man and stays goal-side of him, and an attacker steps off his marker.
  - in build-up and progression a free supporting outfielder steps laterally out of an opponent's passing-lane screen when a bounded nearby angle opens the lane; he retains his tactical depth, onside margin and explicit action orders. An open lane keeps the original support target; this movement never changes a recorded pass or receiver.
  - the nearest eligible free defender remains the presser. Among the remaining same eligible defenders, the one closest to the goal-side cover point covers; a distance tie retains ball-distance then player-index order. Explicit orders/cues, beaten recovery, press zones and the existing cover distance remain authoritative. ← keep already occupied central cover instead of making two defenders exchange it unnecessarily
  - after being beaten by a successful take-on or forward carry, the beaten defender recovers toward his side's shape and does not immediately press the same carrier again for 3 s; another defender may engage while the beaten man recovers.
  - with the ball, supporting players spread and nobody piles onto it: the man on the ball has room around him, a showing teammate offers at a passing distance (about 10–15 m), two players do not stand on each other for seconds, and an opponent beside the man on the ball engages him rather than standing by. Seven or more outfielders within 10 m of the ball are a set piece or a goal-mouth scramble, rarely open play. ← players swarming or sticking together read as playground football, not as a side with a shape
  - the tendencies act in movement (→PLAYER-26): 규율 holds his place, 압박 성향 sets how readily he presses, 예측력 how early he reacts; his positional freedom (→STAFF-46) sets how far his role's habit takes him from his slot. They shape the picture only: the record does not read them.
  - role and trait differences remain observable across comparable situations. Movement follows continuous, speed-bounded paths (→MATCH-12); the four tactics and manager authority remain →MATCH-8/20.
- MATCH-18 [o] Fouls, set pieces, restarts and offside are recorded at their spot's cell and shown as real scenes there. ← set pieces and penalties score about a quarter to a third of real goals

  - free kicks within shooting range may be shot directly or crossed; corners and wide free kicks are delivered into the box.
  - in the picture both sides act through a set piece's preparation, its delivery and the second ball: defenders track attacking runs and protect goal-side space, and nobody freezes because a set piece is in progress (→MATCH-12/17).
  - a receiver of a legal forward pass or free-kick delivery waits onside relative to the live second-last defender and the ball at the kick, then makes his run.

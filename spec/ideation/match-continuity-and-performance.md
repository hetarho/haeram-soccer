# IDEATION match-continuity-and-performance
> st:open@261020 | Make match playback continuous, make players' intentions readable and keep the rest of the world fast.

## vision
The owner reports slow passages while the ball is out of play followed by sudden ball and player movement, players who seem to move separately rather than act as footballers, and slower progression after the engine rebuild. Watching the own club should feel continuous and purposeful. Other fixtures should produce the complete meaningful results and records of a living football world through probability at lower processing cost, with accuracy improved by repeated measurement against the project's target football distributions and detailed play.

- [o] Code inspection identifies continuity risks rather than a reproduced screen defect: `grid/playback.ts` compresses flights to fit the next recorded action, walks restart takers to their spots in at most 0.9 motion seconds and samples player positions eight times per match minute (the ball eighty times). `grid/restarts.ts` places the taker directly at the restart spot, and `grid/shape.ts` places set-piece participants directly. Playback interpolates recorded positions, so fixed pacing alone does not establish physically continuous movement or fix every fast transition.
- [?] Assess continuity through the sequence ball out → retrieval/placement → players reposition → kick → receiver, including minute and period boundaries. Select a speed rule together with a rule for traversing these movements; final displayed coordinates alone are insufficient.

The owner has not selected these examples or a new simulation model.

- [o] The existing summary is lighter than spatial simulation but still runs `playMinute` 90 times per match (120 in extra time), drawing possession, pass counts, chances and shot outcomes. It already returns the common `MatchPlayback`/`MatchRecord` shape, with empty frames and player lines only where players are recorded.

## shape
- flow: compare the request with existing tasks and engine behaviour → identify gaps → owner chooses the scope → update the affected SSOTs → create implementation tasks.
- v1 candidates: predictable selected speed with continuous restart/action transitions; intentions and coordinated reactions that make existing roles and traits visible; cheaper non-own results with complete meaningful numerical records. The owner has selected full probabilistic records and iterative measured calibration for the third item; its tolerances, rollout and performance budget remain open.
- non-own flow: fixture conditions and world state → probabilistic match facts → consistent match result and player/team records → ordinary world settlement and season/career totals. Calibration compares generated outputs with agreed football targets and detailed play, then repeats after adjustments.
- v1 boundary is open: no engine architecture, numerical error threshold, explanatory UI label or change to the meaning of 1x has been selected. Existing sports rules, save compatibility, missing entity coverage and retention costs need assessment before implementation.

## open
- Which reported discontinuities reproduce on the current build, and which come from pacing, compressed actions, sparse player sampling or repositioning? Screen-level reproduction has not been performed in this ideation session.
- Should fixed 1x preserve today's average four seconds per match minute or mean one real second per match second? Fixed tempo also changes how long ordinary build-up and stoppages feel.
- Which coordinated behaviours best convey the desired individual agency, and how much extra own-match processing is acceptable?
- Which complete result fields, material outputs and entity coverage does full parity require? Define scalar records versus retained shot locations, heat maps and pass networks explicitly; the earlier basic-record-only choice is closed.
- Does "the same as actual play" require statistical equivalence across repeated comparable fixtures, or exact per-fixture/per-seed equality? The calibration discussion suggests the former, but the latter has not been explicitly excluded.
- What error bands, sample sufficiency and acceptance criteria should cover means, spread, tails, conditional distributions and record relationships, and what season-time target would make the change worthwhile?
- How should detailed-engine defects be separated from intended football targets in the benchmark, and how will measurements be refreshed when player behaviour changes?
